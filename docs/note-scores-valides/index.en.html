Validated scores in OUCH

What the app could do, what it would not do, and the questions to settle with you.

Working document
14 October 2026
OUCH v0.5.0

OUCH at a glance

A free, open-source pain diary for fibromyalgia and other chronic pain. The person logs their day in under a minute, and their data stays on their phone (an encrypted backup and a code lock are optional). They can generate a report for the appointment, in two versions: a summary for the general practitioner and a detailed report for a pain clinic. The report describes the data without interpreting it.

For discussion

Add validated questionnaire scores to the detailed report, for example the disease activity of spondyloarthritis or the functional impact of low back pain. The aim is that progress between two appointments rests on tools clinicians already know, rather than on our own 0 to 10 scales alone.

What the app would do

  • Record a score with its instrument, date and value.
  • Show it over time in the detailed report, with the limits of the scale.
  • Run a questionnaire that is free of rights, as it is, in a validated version for the person’s language.
  • Offer an optional monthly reminder, never during a flare.

What it would not do

  • Label a score (mild, moderate, severe) or interpret it.
  • Make a diagnosis or advise a treatment.
  • Reproduce, shorten or adapt a questionnaire without its holder’s permission, or keep its name once it is no longer complete.
  • Send data anywhere but where the person puts it themselves.

Proposed approach, in three steps

1. A score log

The person records a score obtained elsewhere, at an appointment for example. A score is not protected: no copyright at stake, and already useful for the report.

2. Roland-Morris in the app

The only questionnaire that can be included without permission, for people who track low back pain.

3. The others, after approval

BASDAI first, then the rest, once the written permission of each holder is obtained.

Five questions for you

  1. Which tools do you actually use at appointments for these conditions, beyond those listed?
  2. Is a number over time enough, or do you need the subscores and the answers?
  3. Does a score calculated by an app, without you having asked the questions, bother you? What would you rather not see?
  4. What frequency is realistic for someone in pain: an optional monthly reminder?
  5. Would a hand-entered score, obtained at an appointment, already be useful to you in the report?

Why not right away

  • A shortened or adapted questionnaire is no longer validated: its properties rest on its items, its wording, its recall period and its scoring.
  • The rhythm is not that of a daily diary: most are filled in once a week or a month, and are long for someone in pain.
  • Rights and translation: clinical or academic use is not use in an app, and a translation often needs the holder’s written agreement.
  • Mood questionnaires (PHQ-9) include a question on suicidal thoughts: OUCH has no way to answer that, and will not include one without.
  • Details: the “validated questionnaires” note in the project’s professionals folder.
